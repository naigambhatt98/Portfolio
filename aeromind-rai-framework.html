What this artifact includes

The page is intentionally focused on the final framework and the PDF slides, without extra portfolio elements.

This project presents a complete Responsible AI framework for AeroMind Robotics, a fictional aerospace robotics company developing AI-supported systems for maintenance, inspection, quality assurance, and operator support.

The framework combines ethical principles, governance structure, roles and responsibilities, accountability timelines, employee training, RAI metrics, AI lifecycle controls, risk evaluation, privacy and compliance review, user education, long-term impact evaluation, and disposition planning.

Core framework areas

Each area is meant to be actionable, measurable, and relevant to robotics and computer vision systems.

🏛️

Governance

Defines executive oversight, RAI review responsibilities, product ownership, and end-user feedback channels.

🔁

Lifecycle Controls

Maps RAI actions across ideation, data collection, development, testing, deployment, monitoring, and retirement.

⚖️

Bias & Fairness

Addresses dataset balance, sensor angle variation, lighting conditions, labeling disagreement, and performance gaps.

🔐

Privacy & Compliance

Covers data minimization, access control, retention, audit logs, customer contract needs, and export control awareness.

👥

Training

Includes role-based RAI training for executives, engineers, ML teams, operators, and customer support teams.

📊

Metrics

Uses measurable controls for safety, reliability, fairness, privacy, training completion, and governance documentation.

🧭

User Education

Defines limitations, trust signals, human override procedures, misuse reporting, and feedback loops.

🌱

Long-Term Impact

Evaluates effects on employees, customers, skill development, workload, safety, reliability, and trust.

Reflection

This project connects robotics and computer vision engineering with the responsibility of building AI systems that are safe, explainable, fair, and useful over time. In high-stakes robotics environments, performance alone is not enough. AI systems also need clear ownership, documented limits, measurable controls, user training, monitoring, and a plan for retraining or retirement when they no longer serve their purpose.
